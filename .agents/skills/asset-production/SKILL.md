# Asset production

Read [the shared asset workflow](../asset-workflow/SKILL.md) and its approval history first. This skill covers the bounded production step between model acceptance and runtime integration: inspect the approved source, make a separate reproducible derivative, and hand it off. It does not authorize production, exports, validation harnesses, game/runtime edits, or integration. Get explicit production scope, output location, expected consumer/view, and validation authority before starting. A bounded pilot authorization may include its disposable validation checks; do not require redundant permission for each mechanical check. Runtime integration and game changes remain separately authorized and approved.

## Preserve and inventory

- Keep the approved `.blend` and its original authoring/generator scripts immutable. Load the detailed model and use Blender operations on a derived copy for reduction, UV work, or baking as appropriate. A separate production script may automate those operations; do not regenerate simpler architecture or characters by changing or rerunning a lower-detail variant of the original generator. Write derived files only to the approved output location. Never silently replace source or substitute a different asset.
- Record source identity/revision, provenance, Blender and tool versions, consumer/library version, reproducible steps, assumptions, and output status in the maintained package's short README or handoff. Pin versions when executing; inspect installed exporter options rather than guessing version-sensitive commands.
- Record a portable assembly contract: axes and units (including what is not established), local origins, whole-scene and per-instance matrices, material roles and emissive intent, and the intended study camera. Apply each glTF transform once; do not reconstruct placement by eye, recenter assets, or repeat an axis conversion already performed by the loader/export. Preserve material-role mapping and instance matrices when batching.
- Inventory objects, UVs/textures and color/channel meaning, and any skins, joints, or animation relevant to the consumer. Record actual screen sizes, population/repetition, Audience and Twilight appearance, and unknown device/performance targets. Ask only for decisions that block the authorized scope; mark other unknowns for the handoff.
- Judge detail at intended screen size and preserve landmark/city skyline silhouette and modular joins. Geometry reduction, normal/AO/base/emissive textures, LOD, and compression are optional: use only when a measured screen-space or delivery benefit justifies them. Do not invent budgets. Normal maps add shading detail, not silhouette; AO can double-darken or become stale; avoid baking fixed lighting into shared base maps. Keep neutral/shared appearance useful under warm Audience and violet Twilight modes; reserve emissive maps for intended self-lit accents.

## Crowd and visual boundary

The production goal is a reduced runtime crowd, not exporting the detailed source crowd unchanged or postponing reduction. At actual on-screen size and intended density, build readable silhouette, pose, and color blocks; detailed faces/anatomy are unnecessary when they do not resolve. Preserve the editable rig and approved character source. Decide from required motion whether the derivative is static posed clusters/mesh instances or retains skeletal animation; verify the chosen path in the actual consumer. Partial occlusion is acceptable, complete hiding is not. A frustum/bounds check proves only that bounds intersect the camera frustum, not that a person is visible; label visual, raycast, or ID-buffer evidence according to what it measures. Do not create a performer/avatar or silently substitute a different crowd design.

glTF does not carry arbitrary Blender node graphs or make a custom toon look automatic. Verify what the actual consumer loader imports for materials, textures, transforms, skins, and animation. Successful GLB geometry/material transfer is separate from accepted appearance in the actual browser consumer. For appearance review, compare paired Audience and Twilight captures with the same geometry, camera, and role mapping, and compare scene pixels rather than only the background. Reflection environments and light scales differ across renderers, so matching numeric light values does not mean matching illumination. If a deliberate runtime toon mapping drops PBR metallic/roughness response, document that as a material-treatment tradeoff, not an exporter defect. Any custom Three.js shader/material behavior is runtime work outside this skill. A Blender beauty render alone is not runtime proof.

## Bounded production and handoff

1. Use a representative pilot only for a new or unproven export/consumer path. Reuse proven endcap, city, crowd, and full-scene patterns without requiring those pilots again; see the [Brightvale pipeline reference](references/brightvale-production-pipeline.md) for concrete examples pinned to their implementation commit.
2. Change one optional production choice at a time. Compare against the unchanged source in the intended view; record what changed, why, and measured evidence. Inspect the exported artifact and actual consumer-loader behavior, including warnings and missing/unsupported material behavior.
3. Report counts with their meaning: distinguish unique geometry/resources from placed totals; multiply geometry per `InstancedMesh.count` for assembled totals. Do not equate linked/reused meshes or scene clones with GPU instancing. Preserve per-instance matrices and material assignments when batching. Keep assembled-scene totals distinct from frame-rendered triangles (which depend on visibility/culling), draw calls, and unique decoded storage. A small regression fixture should check both an ordinary mesh and an instanced mesh with count greater than one. These measurements are not GPU-memory or target-device budgets; state the method and limitations and do not invent thresholds.
4. Deliver a lean maintained package: current derived artifact, reproducible process, concise report, and selected evidence. Before a checkpoint, consolidate useful current artifacts, previews, and reproduction instructions; do not checkpoint every intermediate directory. Keep scratch candidates out of commits by default, but do not delete the only approved evidence or source. Before cleanup, verify final outputs, source/provenance records, and regeneration; then remove only authorized scratch or superseded material. State open decisions and consumer caveats.

## Approval and routing

Preserve the workflow's description, concept, model, and runtime-acceptance gates. After model acceptance, production derivation needs its own review before promotion; do not infer approval from the production authorization or a successful check. Route visual direction to the designer; routine structured production geometry to Sol; complex character/city work to Astra or escalate from Sol. Fixer may do mechanical packaging or runtime work only under the correct authorization, not visual/anatomical production. If an authorized change would redesign geometry/material direction, route it through the existing asset approval gates rather than treating it as routine optimization. Never silently substitute an agent or broaden scope.

See the [production research memo](../../../.lore/work/research/blender-threejs-production-sources.md) for source citations and deeper technical findings; verify rolling documentation and installed versions before relying on version-specific behavior.
