# Brightvale rhythm game concept

## Working name

**Brightvale: Shining Stars** is a proposed working name, not a confirmed title.

## Premise

A rhythm-based game set in Brightvale. The player is one of the **Shining Stars**, a lead performer with an audience, not an audience member or an ordinary citizen learning to become a performer.

The following setting details come from the creator's description of a Brightvale story that was not available during this conversation.

Brightvale sits in a land of perpetual twilight. Outside the city is a force, the Twilight, that seeks to consume everyone. It promises an end to fear and despair, but it can only consume those who allow it in. Simply standing defiant protects a person.

To protect the city, you sing, and the city joins in:

> The power lines strum. The buildings boom. And the wind is chorus.

## Two modes

### Audience mode

You are the lead performer, performing before an audience in Brightvale.

Through these performances, you build:

- Self-esteem.
- Skill.
- Personal resources.

This mode builds the strength that powers your efforts against the Twilight. "Audience mode" means **you have an audience**, not that you watch or support another lead performer.

### Twilight combat mode

You spend what you built in audience mode to power the main challenge: combating the Twilight outside the city, which seeks to consume Brightvale.

You remain the lead performer. Your singing protects the city, and the city itself becomes your accompaniment through its power lines, buildings, and wind.

## Core loop

**Perform for an audience → build self-esteem, skill, and personal resources → spend that strength combating the Twilight → return to perform again.**

The exact progression, spending rules, and rhythm mechanics remain undecided. In particular, whether self-esteem and skill are spent directly or provide persistent benefits has not been settled.

## Design boundaries

- The player is already one of the Shining Stars.
- Both modes center the player as lead performer.
- The Twilight's threat depends on allowing it in, not merely experiencing fear or despair.
- Defiance protects the individual; singing protects the city.
- The city joining the song is central to the concept, not merely background scenery.

## Unconfirmed suggestions from the conversation

These are possible directions, not agreed mechanics:

- Let the city's accompaniment build during a performance, adding power-line strings, building percussion, and wind chorus.
- Express the Twilight's promises of relief through tempting gameplay choices.
- Make holding your ground meaningful independently of perfect rhythm accuracy, so a missed note is not automatically treated as surrender.
- Keep skill and self-esteem persistent while spending supplies or energy. This was an assistant suggestion, not a replacement for the creator's proposed resource loop.

The earlier concepts based on other world collections, and the interpretation of audience mode as attending performances, are superseded and are not part of this concept.
