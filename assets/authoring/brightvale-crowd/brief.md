# Brightvale crowd — approved description

**Description approved:** the user explicitly selected “Approve and generate” for this amended brief. Concept-art generation is authorized. The concept remains a candidate pending separate user approval; modeling and runtime integration are not authorized.

**Selected character direction:** clean cel-shaded anime. Use clear, manageable hair/clothing forms, crisp shape boundaries, and a small number of simple shadow bands. This is the approved direction for the revised concept, not approval of a particular image or a promise that outline effects will transfer through model export. Keep useful color/material regions legible if a later renderer needs to recreate the look; runtime shading feasibility remains unvalidated.

## Purpose and composition

Create a believable audience that makes the highway feel framed by an inhabited city, while keeping the four playable lanes, notes, hit area, existing HUD, and controls easy to read. The crowd occupies the side margins beside the highway and recedes toward its distant end, as in the approved low, dramatic game view. The audience is performing for the player: direct its attention, faces, torsos, and gestures toward the player-side foreground/camera, not toward the distant skyline or an absent stage. The player is the performer; show no separate performer/avatar or stage. Keep the crowd subordinate to gameplay and the city. A decorative outer frame is a separate layer and is not part of this crowd asset.

Show the crowd as its own readable asset/concept and in approximate composites from the common low game camera: beside the existing highway/platform and in front of the approved distant-city concept. In both views and mode treatments, make the audience visibly engage the player at camera-side foreground. Include both warm Audience and violet/darker Twilight treatments. The composites are layout evidence, not approval of the other layers or exact camera values. Keep the highway clear; crop crowd detail before narrowing or obscuring lanes, notes, target area, HUD, or controls.

## Visible reference evidence

The mockups show tightly grouped, human-shaped audience silhouettes along both sides of the highway, with heads and shoulders at varied heights, dense foreground rows, and smaller, less distinct figures receding toward the horizon. Some figures hold narrow, bright glow sticks; warm amber and gold predominate in Audience, while violet, blue, and magenta accents appear in Twilight. The approved distant-city concept's rough composites also show dark audience silhouettes behind lit edge barriers, with a clear central runway. These are visible composition and lighting cues, not established character or clothing lore. The platform concept is a separate, isolated object and supplies no crowd design. **The later user amendment governs orientation:** despite any direction implied by the mockups, the crowd faces and performs toward the player at camera-side foreground.

## Proposed figure treatment (interpretation for approval)

Use convincing, connected human-like bodies with readable head, neck, shoulders, torso, arms, and hands; vary stance, head angle, shoulder line, and scale with perspective. Orient faces, torsos, and gestures toward the player-side foreground/camera. Use natural three-quarter angles suited to figures standing along the highway sides; avoid making every figure identically front-on or turning the crowd toward the distant skyline. The player is the performer, so engagement reads as an audience response to the camera, with no separate visible avatar or stage. Avoid toy-like figures assembled from two cylinders, disconnected bobble heads, or repeated identical silhouettes. At game distance, favor coherent outer contours and a few clear poses over tiny facial detail. Use restrained, varied everyday clothing shapes—such as sleeves, collars, and simple outer layers—as a rendering suggestion only; do not assign uniforms, costumes, specific ages, identities, or species. No face or costume details should compete with the highway. A small number of raised arms and glow sticks can break the crowd outline, without forming a new focal point.

Keep geometry/figure design shared between modes. Change lighting and material response, not crowd membership or silhouette: Audience uses welcoming warm edge light and amber/gold glow sticks; Twilight shifts to darker violet/blue shadow with restrained violet, blue, or magenta accents. Preserve dark, distinct silhouettes against city lights and avoid glow spilling over the gameplay area.

## Review evidence and limits

Alongside the game-distance composition, show a close view of a small representative group so body proportions, connected anatomy, pose variation, simple clothing shapes, and clear orientation toward the player/camera can be judged. Use varied natural three-quarter views rather than a row of identical front-facing figures. Apply the same player-facing orientation in both mode composites; the crowd must not look toward the skyline or an absent stage. This is supporting evidence, not a proposal to enlarge the crowd in play. Do not invent detailed species, character identities, Brightvale lore, or unobserved costume motifs. Exact crowd count, density, camera values, and final materials remain open for concept review. The concept must make the audience feel populated without visual clutter or toy-like construction.
