# Brightvale distant city — approved description

**Description approved: “Approve with spire.”** The distinctive tall distant spire is part of the skyline, not a stage, replacement foreground focal structure, or separate destination. This brief authorizes concept-art generation only. Modeling and runtime integration remain unauthorized and require separate gates.

## Purpose and composition

Create a recognizable, layered city skyline that frames the rhythm-game play area and gives the distant end of the highway a sense of place without becoming a new focal structure. The highway remains dominant in the foreground; the city sits behind it and reads at game-camera distance. The city should feel broad and inhabited, not like a stage or a single isolated building.

The requested scene layers are distinct: (1) distant city architecture, landmarks, and skyline; (2) highway and workflow-test platform; (3) crowd around the playing area; (4) outer screen-edge framing. This asset covers layer 1. Do not add the platform, crowd, or edge framing to the isolated city concept. Also show one rough same-game-camera composite with the approved/existing highway and platform, crowd, and outer framing represented as simple placeholders or existing assets, so city scale and occlusion can be judged in context. The composite is composition evidence, not a claim that those other layers are approved or final.

## Silhouette, architecture, and depth

Use the mockups as style and composition references rather than as a literal blueprint. Their visible city language is a dense, vertically varied fantasy skyline: clustered buildings recede across the horizon, with a distinctive tall, slender distant spire and other towers breaking the roofline; smaller roof and tower forms layer in front of and behind one another. The tall spire belongs within the skyline and must not become a stage, replacement foreground focal structure, or separate destination. The city frames the central playfield rather than blocking it. Keep the far skyline legible as a silhouette, with enough variation in height and spacing to avoid a repeated wall of identical blocks. Use overlapping distance layers and decreasing contrast/detail to suggest depth; keep prominent forms at the sides and preserve a readable opening toward the highway's vanishing region.

The references show illuminated windows and warm architectural lights against twilight, with gold-toned accents and dark blue/violet shadow areas. Carry that material and light character into a cohesive city, not a collection of unrelated glowing signs. Use restrained emissive window/landmark accents and darker structural masses so the skyline remains readable behind gameplay. The mockups include bridges, waterfalls, banners, and ornamental elements; do not treat any one of these as required city content unless selected in concept review. Avoid inventing specific Brightvale landmarks or lore not established by the references.

## Two mode presentations

Evaluate both modes from the same game camera and shared city geometry. Audience is perpetual twilight with warm, welcoming amber/gold light. Twilight is the same twilight scene shifted toward darker violet/purple values and cooler, more uncanny illumination. Show both states in the isolated concept and in the rough composite. Explore the difference through lighting, material response, emission, fog, and restrained effects as appropriate; do not assume post effects alone will create a readable or cohesive change. Keep city contrast and the highway's notes/targets readable in both states. These are two expressions of twilight, not day versus night.

## Exclusions and review evidence

No stage, performer, or replacement focal structure. Do not restore the mockups' score, stars, combo, banners, or other HUD/gameplay decoration as part of the city. Do not let the skyline obscure the highway, crowd, controls, or HUD, or make the city more visually dominant than the playfield. No new setting facts beyond the supplied premise and visible references.

Concept evidence should include: (a) the city by itself in the same camera/projection and approximate framing as gameplay, in Audience and Twilight; and (b) a rough same-camera composite showing how the city relates to the highway/platform, crowd, and outer screen-edge framing in both modes. Label placeholders and unresolved assumptions. Compare against the cited references; do not claim that unseen details are reference-grounded.
